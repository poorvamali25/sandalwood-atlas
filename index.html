THE CENTRAL IDEA

Four questions. One source. Different evidence.

01

What is present?

GC–MS can establish that a compound was detected in a specific sample and method.

02

What reaches air?

Extraction is not the same as headspace emission from the living or intact material.

03

What can humans smell?

Threshold evidence is contextual: matrix, method, panel and stereochemistry matter.

04

What contributes?

GC–O, AEDA, reconstruction, omission and sensory correlation provide different evidence layers.

THE FEATURED SAMPLE

A 15-year-old heartwood snapshot

The featured analytical profile came from mature Santalum album heartwood sampled at IIT Kharagpur, air-dried for 48 hours, solvent extracted, and analyzed by GC–MS. The paper reports a 0.045% volatile yield and 46 identified constituents representing about 93.4% of the volatile fraction.

Those values belong to that experimental context. They are not a universal recipe for sandalwood.

RESEARCH METHOD

How this atlas refuses false certainty.

01

Presence ≠ importance

Never equate “detected” with “important odorant,” and never treat abundance alone as proof of sensory contribution.

02

Matrix-specific thresholds

Thresholds must retain their matrix, units, measurement method and panel context. An air threshold is not automatically interchangeable with a water or oil threshold.

03

OAV is a layer, not a verdict

OAV = concentration / odor threshold, but only when compound identity, matrix, basis, units and conditions are compatible. This edition does not invent numerical OAVs.

04

GC–O and AEDA stay separate

Human olfactory detection at the GC port and dilution-based potency measures provide sensory evidence that should not be collapsed into ordinary GC–MS abundance.

05

Uncertainty is displayed

Missing evidence is labelled as not reported, not measured, unknown or not applicable rather than silently filled with assumptions.
